Q1What idea or question did you start with? Where was the first column or row you were drawn to?
Gordon 的回答
English
I started with a simple question: who is more likely to consult astrology, tarot cards or fortune tellers? My thesis is about AI and fortune-telling (why someone would combine the two instead of using either one alone), so I wanted to see who already looks for answers this way. Pew splits the data into three separate tables, so my first question was how to read all three together. I skipped the religion section at the top because it felt like noise and went to the demographic rows: gender, age, LGBT, income, politics, education and race. The row I was drawn to first was LGBT. It has the widest gap in the whole table: 54% vs 26% for astrology.
中文
我一开始的问题很简单：谁更可能去找占星、塔罗或算命师？我的 thesis 做 AI 加算命（为什么有人要把两者叠在一起，而不是单用其中一个），所以想先看已经在用这种方式找答案的是哪些人。Pew 把数据拆成三张表，我第一个问题就是怎么把三张合起来看。最上面的宗教分类我跳过了，觉得干扰太大，直接看人口分组：性别、年龄、LGBT、收入、政治倾向、教育、种族。最先吸引我的是 LGBT 那一行，全表差距最大：占星 54% 对 26%。

Q4How does the data speak to your emerging thesis problem area (as it stands now)?
Gordon 的回答
English
This data only tells me who consults and how often. It can't tell me whether people believed the answer or acted on it, which is the part my thesis cares about. What it does give me is a place to start looking for interviewees: people who use AI for fortune-telling are probably people who already like fortune-telling. But as soon as I try to narrow it down by group, I run into stereotypes again, so I have to be careful how I use it. My own experience points the same way. I've paid for tarot, astrology charts and bazi readings, but only a few times over several years, always when I felt completely lost, and afterward I forgot what they told me. What mattered was the uncertainty I was in at the time, not the answer.
中文
这份数据只告诉我谁会去、多常去，告诉不了我他们信不信那个答案、有没有照着做，而那正是我 thesis 在意的部分。它给我的是一个找受访者的起点：会用 AI 算命的人，大概本来就爱算命。但我一想按群体缩小范围，就又撞上刻板印象，所以用的时候要小心。我自己的经验也指向同一件事：我花钱算过塔罗、星盘、八字，但几年才一次，都是在特别茫然的时候，算完之后说了什么也忘了。起作用的是当时那种茫然，不是答案本身。

Q2What did you discover that the data was showing you?
Yuxuan 的回答
English
My data shows the steps that lead up to a purchase, but it stops there. It doesn't record what happens afterward. That's actually a lot like how we shop: sometimes we buy something just for the thrill of the moment, and afterward we don't even know ourselves whether we'll use it.
中文
我的数据呈现的是走到购买为止的每一步，但就停在那里，没有记录之后发生什么。这其实很像我们购物的心理：有时候就是图一时之快想买，买完之后用不用，连自己也不知道。
Gordon 对 Yuxuan 的点评
English
Even after going through all the tactics (countdown, scarcity, social proof, one-tap buy), more people in your data still didn't buy than bought. When asked how much influencers affect what they buy, most people answered "a little." That's a neutral answer, and it may not be entirely honest. Read at face value, your chart makes one-tap buy look like the most effective tactic. My takeaway: behind a single purchase decision, the designer has calculated every step to catch you.
中文
你的数据里，就算经过倒计时、稀缺、社会认同、一键购买这一整套，没买的人还是比买的多。问 influencer 对购买影响多大，大多数人答「一点点」，中性答案不一定完全诚实。照表面读，你的图让一键购买看起来最有效。我读出的是：一次购买决策背后，设计者把每一步都算计好了，就为了抓住你。
