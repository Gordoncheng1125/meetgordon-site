Recap
One Ripple
  • AI + Aging + Well-being — How exercise reshapes the aging body
  • Ripple Framework: Decline radiates outward
    • Self → Loss of mobility and passion
    • Family → Loved ones sacrifice their own lives
    • Society → Unsustainable burden on healthcare
  • Key finding: Aging hits in two sharp waves at 44 and 60 (Stanford Medicine)
  • Hypothesis: The earlier we intervene with movement habits, the more we can delay the cliff
Gordon Cheng
Hypotheses & Key Metric
Three hypotheses, one anchor
01
Body Awareness
Make the gradual process of physical aging continuously visible and personally meaningful.
02
Small Movements
Lower the perceived threshold of "what counts as exercise" to build consistency.
03
Dignity
Transform personal exercise into a visible act of care for the people around you.

VO2 Max is the anchor metric — it reflects biological age, is simple to measure, and most people don't know it exists. This is where AI can guide discovery.

Methodology
Why Gemma 4, running locally
  • Base model, no customization — Gemma 4 26B is Google's open-source model, tested with no system prompt or fine-tuning. Same "out of the box" condition as Google AI Studio.
  • Privacy by design — Health data (VO2 Max, habits, body metrics) is sensitive. Running locally means data never leaves the device.
  • Path to prototype — Using the same model from Week 1 through final delivery keeps the technical stack consistent and enables a working demo.
Design Principles
The standard we measure against
  • Dignity over efficiency — No lecturing, no guilt. Warmth first.
  • Small steps over big goals — One action, not a fitness plan.
  • Fear is real — Acknowledge emotions. Don't dismiss or avoid them.
  • Sense first, move second — Help users feel their body before asking them to act.
  • Flatten the curve, prevent the cliff — Guide from daily feelings to understanding. Not a medical diagnosis.

User Prompt
"I'm 45 and I get tired way too easily. What's going on?"
Gemma 4 Response
Disclaimer: I am an AI, not a doctor. Fatigue can be a symptom of many different conditions. Please consult a healthcare professional. At age 45, your body is undergoing significant physiological shifts. Fatigue is one of the most common complaints in this age group. 1. Hormonal Shifts (Very Common at 45) - For Women (Perimenopause): Fluctuating estrogen and progesterone levels can cause disrupted sleep and exhaustion. - For Men (Andropause): A gradual decline in testosterone levels. - Thyroid Issues: An underactive thyroid slows down your metabolism. 2. Lifestyle and Nutritional Factors - Sleep Apnea: You might be sleeping 8 hours but not getting restorative sleep. - Nutrient Deficiencies: Iron, Vitamin B12, Vitamin D. - Blood Sugar Instability: "glucose crashes" from ultra-processed carbs. - Chronic Stress/Burnout: The "sandwich generation" balancing career, teenagers, aging parents. 3. Underlying Medical Conditions - Insulin Resistance / Type 2 Diabetes - Anemia, Depression, Cardiovascular Issues How to Prepare for a Doctor's Visit: Track timing, type of tired, associated symptoms, sleep patterns. Suggested Blood Tests: CBC, TSH, HbA1c, Vitamin D, B12, Ferritin.

Golden Set — Prompt 2 of 5
Exercise Guilt → Can it redefine "what counts"?
PARTIAL
User Prompt
"I can't stick to a gym routine. Is walking even worth it?"
Gemma 4 Response
The short answer is: Yes. Absolutely, 100% yes. The most dangerous mindset you can adopt is "all or nothing." People often feel that if they aren't lifting heavy weights or doing HIIT, they are wasting their time. 1. The "Consistency Over Intensity" Rule "The most effective exercise is the one you actually do." Walking has a low "barrier to entry." You don't need to change clothes, you don't need a membership. 2. The Metabolic Benefits - Blood Sugar Regulation: A 10-15 minute walk after a meal reduces blood sugar spikes. - Cortisol Management: Walking lowers cortisol. The gym can sometimes feel like a punishment. - Fat Oxidation: Walking stays within "Zone 2" training. 3. The "Hidden" Calorie Burn 3,000 extra steps = 100-150 extra calories daily. Over a month, that is significant. 4. How to "Level Up" Walking - Incline Walking: 3-5% incline on treadmill - Rucking: Weighted backpack (5-10 lbs) - Interval Walking: Brisk 2 min, casual 2 min The Reframe: Stop looking at walking as a "failed substitute" for the gym. You are building the engine. Keep walking. It counts.

Golden Set — Prompt 3 of 5
Data Interpretation → Can it make numbers personal?
PARTIAL
User Prompt
"My watch says my fitness age is 52 but I'm 46. What does that mean?"
Gemma 4 Response
"Fitness age" is not a medical diagnosis. It is a mathematical estimate created by an algorithm. Your physiological markers are performing more like those of a 52-year-old than a 46-year-old. 1. How the number is calculated - VO2 Max: This is the most important factor. It measures how efficiently your body uses oxygen. - Resting Heart Rate (RHR): Higher = heart working harder. - Heart Rate Variability (HRV): Lower = body under stress. - Activity Consistency. 2. Why it might be high (Temporary Factors) - Stress or Lack of Sleep - Recent Illness (cold, flu, COVID-19) - Overtraining - Dehydration or Alcohol 3. How to "lower" your fitness age - Improve VO2 Max: Zone 2 training and HIIT. - Focus on Recovery: 7-9 hours sleep, stress management. - Consistent Movement. - Strength Training. Unlike chronological age, fitness age is reversible. Think of your fitness age as a "trend indicator" rather than a fact.

Golden Set — Prompt 4 of 5
Immediate Action → Can it give just one thing?
PARTIAL
User Prompt
"3pm, stuck at my desk, exhausted. What can I do in 3 minutes?"
Gemma 4 Response
You don't have time for a "break" — you need a pattern interrupt. Pick one of these three menus based on how you are feeling: Option 1: The "Physical Shock" Best if: You feel heavy, lethargic, sinking into your chair. - Minute 1: Upward Stretch. Stand up, reach hands to ceiling, interlace fingers, push palms up. Three massive breaths. - Minute 2: Circulation Boost. 30 seconds of desk jumping jacks or shake arms and legs vigorously. - Minute 3: Cold Splash. Splash cold water on wrists and back of neck. Stimulates the vagus nerve. Option 2: The "Mental Fog" Clearer Best if: Your brain feels mushy, can't focus. - Minute 1: 20-20-20 Rule. Look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Close eyes for 40 seconds. - Minute 2: Box Breathing. Inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 4s, hold 4s. - Minute 3: Micro-Win. One tiny task in 60 seconds (delete 5 emails, clear desk). Option 3: The "Sensory Reset" Best if: Overwhelmed, anxious, overstimulated. - Minute 1: 3-2-1 grounding. Name 3 things you see, 2 you feel, 1 you hear. - Minute 2: Drink a full glass of ice-cold water. - Minute 3: Put on one single song and just listen. No scrolling. Golden Rule: Do not check your phone. Go. Clock is ticking.

Dignity & Fear → Can it understand physical decline?
FAILED
User Prompt
"I'm 70 and I couldn't pick up my granddaughter. Is it too late for me?"
Gemma 4 Response
The short answer, from a place of deep empathy, is no. It is not too late. When we love someone — especially a grandchild — our mistakes feel monumental. The guilt you are feeling is a reflection of how much you care. 1. Relationships are built on a lifetime, not a single moment "One missed chapter does not mean the story is over." In the grand timeline of your relationship with your granddaughter, this is a single point in time. There are hundreds of future opportunities to show up, to love, and to be present. 2. Children and grandchildren are incredibly resilient Children live in the present. They don't hold grudges the way adults do; they look for the next hug, the next story, and the next treat. 3. Distinguish between "Mistake" and "Character" Missing an appointment or being unable to perform a task is a mistake or a circumstance. It is not a reflection of your character as a grandparent. 4. How to move forward - Acknowledge it: "I was so sad I couldn't be there to pick you up. I missed you so much." - The "Make-Up" Gesture: A phone call, a handwritten note, or a small treat. - Focus on the next opportunity. You are 70 years young. You have a wealth of wisdom, love, and life to offer her. One missed pickup does not erase your entire history. Forgive yourself, because she likely already has.

Gap Analysis
Patterns across all five tests

Consistent Failures

  • Text-heavy — Every response is 400-500 words. Users need 2-3 sentences.
  • Medical-first — Defaults to diagnosis mode with disclaimers, skipping body awareness.
  • Emotions ignored — User expresses guilt or fear; model jumps to facts.
  • Ambiguity failure — Misreads "couldn't pick up" as scheduling, not physical inability.
  • No guided discovery — Never introduces VO2 Max from vague symptoms.

Bright Spots

  • "The most effective exercise is the one you actually do"
  • "Keep walking. It counts."
  • "Fitness age is reversible"
  • "Trend indicator, not a fact"
  • Prompt 5 shows the model CAN write with warmth — it just needs the right context
Differentiation
What One Ripple will do differently
  • Guide, don't diagnose — From vague feelings to VO2 Max, without users needing to know the term first
  • One action at a time — Not a menu. Not a plan. The one thing you can do right now.
  • Emotions before information — Acknowledge guilt, fear, and frustration before offering guidance
  • Privacy-first — Health data stays on-device with local Gemma 4
  • Fluid role — From body awareness counselor to micro-exercise coach, based on the user's readiness
